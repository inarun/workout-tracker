Privacy policy

Ledger — a workout tracker for iPhone and the web.

Effective 31 October 2026 (last updated 31 October 2026)

This policy covers the Ledger iPhone app and the Ledger web app. Each app also has a short version in Settings. If they ever disagree, email the address below and this page will be corrected. Using Ledger is also covered by its Terms of Use.

In short

  • No ads, no tracking, and no analytics or crash-reporting code. Your data is never sold.
  • Without an account, Ledger sends your log to no server, except to the web app's Google Drive backup script, if you set one up.
  • With an account, your log is synced through Google Firebase, and only for you.
  • What Ledger reads from Apple Health (your heart rate and energy burned during a workout it records) stays on your iPhone and Apple Watch. It is never uploaded or used for advertising.
  • The Apple Intelligence, voice and photo features run on your iPhone.
  • Ledger is a training log, not medical advice.

Who is responsible

Ledger is made and run by one person, as an individual, not a company. Contact: support@nusayb.me.

Cloud sync is optional

Without an account, everything you log (workouts, templates, body weight, exercise photos, settings) is saved only on your device: in the iPhone app's storage, or in your browser's storage for the web app. Ledger sends it to no server. On iPhone it can still reach your own devices and Apple's services: your paired Apple Watch gets the workout in progress, Apple Health gets what you turn on (see On iPhone), and your iPhone's own iCloud or computer backups include the app's files, as Apple sets them up.

The web app loads its UI framework (React) from Cloudflare's CDN. Cloudflare sees your IP address and browser, as with any website, and nothing about your training. If you set up the web app's optional Google Drive backup script, it copies your log to your own Google account: the current file, the previous copy and up to 90 daily restore points, in a folder in your Drive. The developer cannot access them, and deleting your Ledger account does not touch them; delete them in Google Drive.

What is stored

With an account, which keeps the same log on each device you sign in on, the following is stored in the cloud:

  • Your training records: workouts (including notes you write and workouts you deleted recently), templates, body weight, your custom exercises and changes to exercise details, and app settings.
  • Your email address and an account ID. If you use a password, only a hash of it is kept.
  • If you sign in with Google: the ID, name and profile picture Google shares. If you sign in with Apple: an ID and your email, or Apple's private relay address if you hide it. Ledger does not ask Apple for your name.

The workouts, notes and body weight you enter in Ledger are your log, whether or not you also save them to Apple Health, and with an account they sync like the rest of it. To sign you in, the sign-in service also processes your IP address and device or browser details. Exercise photos, the workout you haven't saved yet, and anything Ledger reads from Apple Health are never uploaded. Signing out stops syncing.

On iPhone

These features work only when you use them. iOS asks your permission before Ledger can use Health, the camera or the microphone.

  • Apple Health. If you turn it on in Settings > Apple Health, Ledger saves your workouts and body weight to Health, a lift as strength training and an activity as its own type, such as running or yoga. With Record Heart Rate on, it also records a workout with Health, saving the energy burned and reading your heart rate during it. With the Apple Watch app, if you allow Health access on the watch, the watch records each new workout it shows, with the heart rate and energy burned from its sensors, and saves it to Health when you finish on your iPhone. The workout passes between your iPhone and your watch directly, not through a server. It reads back only its own entries and the heart rate of the workouts it recorded, to show them in the app, and, with Use Sleep in Plans on, last night's sleep. What Ledger reads from Health stays on your iPhone and Apple Watch: it is not saved in Ledger's own files, is never uploaded, and is never used for advertising, marketing or data mining. Ledger never writes false data to Health.
  • Camera and photos. Used to take exercise reference photos, which stay on your iPhone. Log from a Photo reads a written workout or a screenshot, and Machine Is This? reads a photo of a gym machine, both on your iPhone. You take these photos or choose them from your library, and they are not kept.
  • Microphone and speech. Say a Set turns your voice into text on your iPhone, with Apple's on-device speech recognition. The audio is not kept or sent.
  • Apple Intelligence (iOS 27 and later; Settings > Apple Intelligence Features). Ask Ledger, workout summaries, last week and block reviews, Log from a Photo, Machine Is This?, Say a Set, New Template, progression hints and Tidy Names use Apple's on-device model. Ask Ledger reads what you type, then plans the workout or answers the question from your own log on your iPhone. Your questions, the workout details they use (including heart rate), and the answers stay on your iPhone. Ask can search your own notes, on your iPhone. What you say hurts stays on your iPhone so plans spare it for 3 days, and is removed the next time you open Ledger after that. With Use Sleep in Plans on, Ledger reads last night's sleep from Health on your iPhone, only to make a plan lighter, and does not keep it. With the Apple Watch app, what you say or scribble on the watch goes to your iPhone to be read.
    Ledger sends nothing to any AI service.
  • Spotlight, Siri, widgets and Live Activities. Each workout's name, date, exercises and number of sets (for an activity, its time, distance and intervals), and each exercise's name, how many workouts it is in and when it was last done, are indexed on your iPhone so Spotlight and Siri can find them. Siri answers questions about your training only once your iPhone is unlocked, and says the answer aloud; Ask Ledger words it with Apple's on-device model, on your iPhone. Widgets and the Lock Screen show your recent training and, during a recorded workout, your heart rate. The workout's Live Activity, heart rate included, also shows in your Apple Watch's Smart Stack and, while your iPhone is connected to a car, on the CarPlay Dashboard. Rest timer alerts are local notifications, on your iPhone and, if you allow them there, on your watch; Rest Alarm, if you turn it on, sets an alarm on your iPhone for the end of each rest. Ledger sends none of this to a server. Siri itself is Apple's, and Apple's privacy policy covers what it does with a request.
  • Backups, exports and the stats-card images you save to Photos are files you create and share yourself.

What it is for

Only to run the features above: keeping your log on your devices and restoring it if you lose one. Your data is not used to profile you, not used for advertising, and not sold or shared for any other purpose.

Where it is kept

Your synced data is stored with Google Firebase (Authentication and Cloud Firestore), in a project owned by the developer. Google processes it only on Ledger's behalf, under Firebase's data processing terms, and gives it the same or equal protection as this policy. Its servers are global, so your data may be stored outside your country, including in the United States. Server rules let only your own signed-in, verified account read or write your records. The developer can reach the project through Google's console to run the service, and for nothing else.

Apple and Google see that you used their sign-in with Ledger, but not your training. Firebase is the only service that stores your log for Ledger (see Other parties), and none of it goes to an AI service.

Other parties

No advertising, analytics or social-media company collects information through Ledger. Using Ledger does mean your device contacts a few services, each under its own privacy policy. Like any server, each sees your IP address and basic device or browser details.

  • GitHub Pages hosts the web app and this page.
  • Cloudflare (cdnjs) serves the web app's UI framework, React. It is told nothing about you or your training.
  • Google Firebase runs accounts and sync, and only if you create an account.
  • Google or Apple, if you sign in with one of them, confirms who you are.
  • Google Drive, only if you set up the web app's backup script, keeps that backup in your own Google account.
  • Apple runs the App Store, TestFlight, and the iPhone features Ledger uses, such as Siri and Health.

Of these, only Firebase stores your training log for Ledger; a Drive backup is your own copy in your own account. A link you tap to another website, such as an exercise's source, opens that site in your browser, under its own policy.

How long it is kept

Cloud records are kept until you delete them or your account. A deleted workout is kept for at least 7 days so you can restore it (Recently Deleted on iPhone, Deleted workouts on the web). After that, Ledger removes it for good the next time it opens, and from the cloud when a signed-in device next syncs. When a record is deleted for good, a small deletion marker remains so your other devices remove it too. The marker keeps only the record's type, its key (an ID, a date, or an exercise or setting name) and when it was deleted and synced, and it stays until you delete your account. After account deletion, Firebase removes the remaining sign-in data within 180 days and keeps IP addresses for a few weeks.

Deleting it

You can delete your data or withdraw your consent at any time.

  • Delete your account in the app: Settings > Account > Delete Account on iPhone, or Settings > Cloud Sync > Delete account on the web. This deletes all your cloud records and then the account.
  • When you delete your account on iPhone, Ledger also revokes its Sign in with Apple access, and tells you if that fails. Deleting on the web can't do this: remove Ledger in Settings > your name > Sign-In & Security > Sign in with Apple on an Apple device.
  • The log on your device stays until you delete it or the app. Data saved to Apple Health stays in Health; delete it in the Health app. A Google Drive backup stays in your Drive until you delete it there. To remove Ledger's access to your Google account, go to your Google Account's third-party connections.
  • To withdraw permission, sign out, turn features off in Ledger's Settings, or change Health, Camera and Microphone access in iPhone Settings.
  • If you can't reach the button, email support@nusayb.me from the address on the account and it will be deleted for you.

No analytics, no ads

Ledger contains no analytics or crash-reporting code. There is no tracking, no advertising and no third-party analytics SDK in either app. Your data is never sold, rented or shared for marketing.

Apple may share crash reports from the iPhone app with the developer and, if you allow it in iPhone Settings (Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements), usage statistics. If you test Ledger through TestFlight, Apple also shares your name or email and any feedback you send. The developer uses these only to fix problems.

Do Not Track

Ledger does not track you over time or across other websites or apps, and lets no one else do so through it. It treats every visit the same whether or not your browser sends Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control.

Your rights

Wherever you live, you can ask for a copy of your data, a correction, deletion, a portable copy, or for its use to be restricted. Email support@nusayb.me. There is no charge. You can also take a copy of your log yourself at any time: Settings > Backup and Export > Export Backup on iPhone, or Settings > Data > Export JSON on the web. Where the law requires a legal basis, it is your consent, given by creating an account and withdrawn by signing out or deleting it. You can also complain to your data protection authority.

California residents

Ledger does not sell or share personal information, as the California Consumer Privacy Act defines those words, and has not done so in the past 12 months. It uses your account login and fitness data only to provide the features you use. You can ask to know what Ledger holds about you, to correct it or to delete it, and you won't be treated differently for asking. You can take a copy at any time (see Your rights). To make a request, email support@nusayb.me from the address on your account, or have someone you have authorized in writing do so. You will get an answer within 45 days.

Children

Ledger is not directed at children, and accounts are not knowingly created for anyone under 16. The iPhone app asks you once to confirm that you are at least 16; it doesn't ask for a date of birth, check the answer or keep an age. The web app does not ask anyone's age. If you believe a child has made an account, email the address above and it will be deleted. Anyone under 18 can remove what they have logged, or their account, at any time: delete workouts in History, delete the account in Settings, or email the address above and it will be done for them.

Not medical advice

Ledger is a training log, not medical advice. Check with a doctor or other qualified professional before starting a new program, and stop if something hurts.

Changes

When what Ledger collects or how it is used changes, this page and the notices in the apps are updated, along with the dates at the top. Before a change that collects something new or uses data in a new way takes effect, the app will tell you.
